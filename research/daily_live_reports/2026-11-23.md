- **Amethyst (simulated points):** put_wall_bounce long 645.48 → 648.00 (+2.52), bullish_magnet longs stopped (−0.80 and −1.76), and a put_wall_bounce long at 650.85 still open at the close.

- **SPY daytrader and swing 30m:** no QQQ activity.

**Why intraday structure could not trade the range**

1. **A stale armed short setup blocks fresh shorts.**
   - `QQQ:short:gamma_structural_level_rejection` has been ARMED since **2026-11-12 11:24 ET**, with its pivot at ~693.9 and invalidation at 694.11.
   - Price never went back above 694, and every confirmation is refused because the risk is 26–185 ATR. The setup can neither enter nor invalidate; it has logged 1,746 abstentions so far.
   - `QQQ:short:trend_pullback_continuation` is stuck the same way (invalidation 741.07).
   - Setup ids are unique per ticker, direction and detector, so no fresh short could arm at 652–653. ARMED has no age limit.
2. **Each setup gets one try per day.**
   - A CLOSED setup is revived only when its candidate is re-registered. The QQQ long candidate was last refreshed at 04:00.
   - The long rejection, pullback and VWAP-reclaim setups that closed at 09:48–09:52 never came back.
   - Since 11-02, only 264 of 4,251 setups closed during RTH (6.2%) were detected again the same day.
3. **Discovery measured the gap against a stale price.**
   - Discovery uses `shared_universe.csv` `px` as `prior_close`. That column is a median 19.7% off the last close across the top 750 eligible names, and 84% are more than 5% off.
   - QQQ's px is 742.60 against an actual prior close of 608.45, so QQQ was promoted as a **−11.7% gap-down short** on a +7% gap up.
4. **The VWAP exit kills range fades by construction.**
   - The exhaustion check runs on every RUNNING setup and closes a long once it is more than 0.25 ATR below VWAP.
   - VWAP sits mid-range, so a long bought at the floor exits on its first managed bar.

**Would fixing those make money? Not by itself.** I tested the idea directly
(`research/range_fade_2026-11-23/`): fade the outer 20% of a causal 60-minute balance envelope, enter at the next
bar's open, and target the midline or the far edge. Controls were a breakout mirror and random-time entries with the
same bracket.

- **Rule as tested, QQQ 1m 2024-02..2026-11 (706 sessions):** −0.06 bps per trade gross (n 2,774, standard error 0.42, MDE80 1.2). It is flat in every year and the same as both controls.
- **Your exact condition, "already bounced at least twice":** −0.04 bps (midline target) and +0.48 ± 0.76 bps (far-edge target).
- **SPY, same rule:** +0.24 ± 0.56 bps.
- **What it means:** a minimum detectable effect of about 1.2 bps is below the cost of a round trip, so this is a rejection at this power, not an underpowered null. Where price sits inside a 1-minute balance envelope does not predict which edge is hit first.
- **Today in isolation:** the rule made +141 bps gross (far-edge target, 7 trades, 5 hit target). Its first trade (11:17, −62 bps) was stopped when the earlier 650–657 envelope broke. Today is the kind of day the eye remembers; the rule cannot know beforehand which balance will hold.

**What is actually missing is a regime input.** The candidate is dealer gamma: when dealers are long gamma they hedge against moves, which pins price.
- **Data:** 80 sessions of archived QQQ chain ladders (06-17..11-23), joined causally to each trade.
- **Result:** the fade did better with positive GEX than negative in all four variants. Example: far-edge target with the touch condition gave +5.41 bps vs −3.01 (n 59 vs 88); the differences are +3 to +8 bps.
- **Not measurable yet:** MDE80 is 5–10 bps per cell, and the variants share trades.
- **Next step:** pool the SPY, IWM and SPX snapshot history to roughly 3–4× the trade count. Pre-register the GEX+ minus GEX− fade difference with the random and breakout controls. Only then add a paper-shadow detector, and only for positive-gamma regimes.
